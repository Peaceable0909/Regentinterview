Complete Interview Preparation Guide

Everything you need to know, in the order you should learn it. Read all sections before your interview.

1. Understanding the Purpose of the Interview ▼

A credibility interview is the university's way of checking that you are genuinely prepared for your studies, understand what you are coming to study, and can realistically support yourself in the UK. The goal is not to trick you. They just want to be sure you know the basics about your course, your accommodation, your finances, your future plans, and why you chose Regent.

If you answer confidently, naturally, and truthfully, you will pass. Your answers should sound like things you discovered through your own research — not like a memorised script.

A. Your Campus and Address ▼

Regent will expect you to know exactly which campus your course is taught at and the full address including postcode. The campus depends on your programme. Check the What to Know tab for your specific address.

Examples of campuses: Wembley, Holborn, Great Titchfield Street, or Southall — depending on your programme. Learn the full street address and postcode, not just the area name.

B. Why You Chose Regent College London ▼

Make this personal but grounded in real facts. Key reasons to mention:

  • Regent teaches in small class sizes, so you get personal academic and career support.
  • The college has a 93% student satisfaction rate in the National Student Survey.
  • Degrees are awarded by the University of Bolton / University of Greater Manchester, ranked 28th in the UK.
  • They provide free extra training: Thinking Into Character (TIC) — a leadership, entrepreneurship, and goal-setting programme.
  • Free laptop loan scheme.
  • Strong student development services and career support.

Your answer should sound like you discovered these benefits through your own research — not like you are reading from a list.

C. What Extras Regent Offers Beyond Academics ▼

Be ready to talk about more than just your course. Mention:

  • Thinking Into Character (TIC) — a free programme covering leadership, entrepreneurship, goal-setting, and how to be successful in the workplace.
  • Student Development Services — support for your academic journey and personal growth.
  • Career support — help preparing for work after graduation.
  • Free laptop loan — the college lends laptops to students who need them.

These extras show the interviewer that you understand Regent is more than just classroom learning.

D. Your Accommodation Details ▼

They expect exact details — not vague answers. You should know:

  • The specific area of London you will stay in.
  • The full postcode (e.g. HA9 3BQ or N1 7GJ).
  • How much rent costs per month.
  • Why you chose that area — proximity to campus, good transport links, fits your budget.

Show that you researched using sites like SpareRoom and that the cost fits within your monthly budget.

E. Modules in Your Course ▼

This is extremely important. You must name at least 3 modules, explain why they interest you, and connect them to your background or career goals.

You should also compare your course at Regent with at least two similar courses at other UK universities, and explain why you still chose Regent.

Download and read the course specification from the University of Bolton website. Find the link in the Resources tab. Never say you do not know how the course is structured — they expect you to have done this research.

F. How Your Course Is Assessed ▼

From the course specification, understand the assessment methods. Depending on your course this could include:

  • Written assignments and coursework
  • Group projects and presentations
  • Written exams
  • Case studies
  • A final dissertation or business project

Never say you do not know — they expect you to have looked at the course specification online before the interview.

G. Your Future Plans After Graduation ▼

Your answer must be specific and must show that you plan to return home and apply your skills there. Include:

  • Short-term goals — the first role you will apply for after graduation, with a specific job title.
  • Long-term plans — where you see yourself in 5 years, possibly a management role or your own business.
  • Actual companies you would target — name real organisations in your sector.
  • Expected salary range — in GBP. Research realistic salaries for that role and location.
  • How you will use the skills from your degree back in your home country.
H. Your Financial Preparation ▼

Interviewers want to be sure you will not face financial difficulty in the UK. You should know:

  • The exact tuition fee for your course.
  • The cost of living in London — use £1,200 to £1,600 per month as a guide, broken down into rent, food, transport, and utilities.
  • The total amount you have available, converted into both GBP and your local currency. Use Oanda for this.
  • Who is funding you — yourself, family, sponsor — and their income or savings.
  • How you will cover unexpected expenses.

Use the LSE London budget guide and the Which.co.uk student budget tool to prepare accurate figures.

I. Visa Rules and Responsibilities ▼

You should know all of the following without hesitation:

  • You can work a maximum of 20 hours per week during term time.
  • You can work full-time during official university holidays.
  • You cannot claim public funds.
  • You must attend all scheduled classes.
  • You must notify the university if you change your address.

They want to check that you understand the responsibilities that come with a student visa before you arrive.

J. What You Have Been Doing Since Your Last Qualification ▼

Keep this simple and honest. Cover:

  • Any work experience or employment since your last degree or qualification.
  • Internships or voluntary work.
  • Skills you have been developing.
  • Training or short courses you have completed.
  • Your responsibilities in your current or most recent job.

Avoid leaving unexplained gaps. If there was a period where you were not working or studying, explain what you were doing — caring for a family member, dealing with a personal situation, preparing for this application, etc.

K. Health Conditions ▼

If you have no health issues, simply say so clearly: "I have no health conditions that would affect my studies."

If you do have a health condition, briefly explain how you manage it and reassure the interviewer that it will not impact your attendance or academic performance. You do not need to go into unnecessary detail.

Final Tips to Pass

Speak slowly and calmly. Do not rush.
Answer naturally, as if talking to a real person, not reading from memory.
Be honest about your finances and your intentions at all times.
Show that you researched Regent and London life fully.
Stay consistent — every answer must match what is on your application.

    Sample Natural Answers

    These are model answers written in a calm, natural style. Adapt them using your own details — never recite them word for word. The interviewer wants to hear your story, not a prepared speech.

    QWhy did you choose the UK for your studies?
    "I chose the UK because the education system is practical, respected worldwide, and allows students to gain real career skills. The UK also has a diverse environment, so it is easy to meet people from different cultures. I felt the teaching style would help me grow academically and professionally."
    Adapt this by adding something specific to your field — for example, "The UK is a global hub for finance / technology / healthcare, which is directly relevant to my career goals."
    QWhy Regent College London specifically?
    "I chose Regent because of their small class sizes and strong student support system. The 93% satisfaction rate and the fact that degrees are awarded by a well-ranked UK university gave me confidence in the quality of the programme. I also liked the Thinking Into Character leadership programme and the free laptop loan — it shows the college genuinely invests in its students beyond the classroom."
    Make it personal. Mention something specific you found during your research — for example, a particular module, the campus location, or the student development services.
    QHow does your course relate to your background and previous experience?
    "My previous degree was in [subject] and I have worked in [field] for [X] years. This course builds directly on that foundation — particularly the modules on [Module A] and [Module B], which cover areas I want to develop further. The combination of theory and practical application in this programme is exactly what I need to move into [next career step]."
    Fill in the brackets with your real details. Connect at least two specific modules to your actual background. Be concrete, not vague.
    QWhere will you be staying and how much will it cost?
    "I will be living in [area, e.g. Wembley], with a postcode of [e.g. HA9 7XX]. I researched accommodation on SpareRoom and found a room for approximately £[750] per month. I chose this area because it is close to my campus, well-connected by the London Underground, and the cost fits within my monthly budget."
    You must have a specific area and postcode ready. If you have not found accommodation yet, research SpareRoom now and pick a realistic option near your campus so you can speak to it confidently.
    QHow will you fund your studies?
    "My studies are funded by [myself / my family / my sponsor]. The total tuition fee is £[X]. I estimate my monthly living costs at around £[1,300], covering rent of £[750], food of approximately £[250], transport of around £[130], and other utilities and expenses. I currently have savings of £[X] which I have already converted to GBP using Oanda. My [parent / sponsor] also earns approximately [£X / local currency equivalent] per year and will contribute to my living costs. I also have a contingency fund of £[X] for unexpected expenses."
    The more specific your figures, the more credible you sound. Know the exact tuition fee. Know the exchange rate. Have the breakdown ready before the interview.
    QWhat are your goals after graduation?
    "In the short term, I plan to return to [home country] and apply for a position as [job title] at companies such as [Company A] or [Company B]. I expect a starting salary in the range of [local currency equivalent]. In the long term, I want to [e.g. move into a management role / establish my own business / lead a team in my sector] within five years. This course will give me the specific skills in [area] that are currently in high demand in my country."
    The interviewer needs to be confident you plan to return home. State this clearly and give a concrete plan for what you will do when you get back.
    QDo you understand your visa responsibilities?
    "Yes. As an international student on a student visa, I know I can work a maximum of 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official university holidays. I must attend all scheduled classes and lectures. I am required to notify the university if I change my address. I also understand that I cannot claim public funds during my stay in the UK. I take these responsibilities seriously."
    Say this clearly and without hesitation. If you stumble on the working hours limit (20 hours), the interviewer will notice. Know it by heart.
